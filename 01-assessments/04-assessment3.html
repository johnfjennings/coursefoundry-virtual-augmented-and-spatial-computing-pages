Assessment 3

Individual Project — Interactive Immersive Application

Virtual and augmented reality, also known as immersive reality, is any technology that “replicates an environment, real or imagined, and simulates a user’s physical presence and environment to allow for user interaction. Spatial computing enables immersive interaction by understanding the physical world, knowing and communicating our relation to places in that world, and navigating through those places.
Author

Mr. John Jennings

Overview

Assessment 3 is the capstone of this module. You will design, build, test and evaluate an original interactive immersive application for VR, AR, or a spatial computing platform.

This is where everything from the semester comes together: the hardware, the interaction design, the perceptual and comfort principles, and the evaluation methods. You choose the concept; you are assessed on how well you execute and justify it.

Item Detail
Weighting 40%
Type Individual project
Submission Individual
Due End of semester

Learning Outcomes

This assessment covers all four module outcomes:

  • LO1: Critical understanding of 3D systems and AR/VR principles in a spatial computing context
  • LO2: Explore, critique and deconstruct modern VR, AR and spatial computing experiences
  • LO3: Theoretical understanding of, and practical competence with, advanced input/output devices and sensors
  • LO4: Apply HCI principles to the construction and evaluation of immersive systems

The Brief

Build an interactive immersive application on one of the platforms used in this module.

Your project must:

  • run on real hardware, not only in the editor
  • support meaningful user interaction beyond looking around
  • make deliberate, defensible design choices about comfort, input and spatial layout
  • be evaluated with real users, however small the sample

The scope is yours to set. A small, polished, well-evaluated project scores higher than an ambitious broken one. Depth beats breadth.

Example directions

  • a training or procedural simulation
  • a spatial data or information tool
  • an AR experience anchored to real surfaces or objects
  • an accessibility-focused interaction study
  • an experimental or artistic immersive piece

Talk to me before committing if you are unsure whether your idea has enough substance.

Milestones

These are checkpoints, not separate submissions. They exist to stop projects failing late.

Point What you should have
Week 6 Concept agreed and platform chosen
Week 9 Core interaction working on device
Week 11 Testable build, ready for peer evaluation
Week 12 Evaluation complete, improvements applied

Deliverables

  1. Application build — an installable or runnable build for the target device, plus project source files.
  2. Demonstration video — 3 to 5 minutes, captured through the lens or in first-person, showing the application actually running.
  3. Project report — 2500 to 3000 words.
  4. Evaluation evidence — your test materials, notes or questionnaire responses.

Report structure

  1. Concept and rationale
  2. Design decisions, with reference to module concepts
  3. Technical implementation
  4. Evaluation method and findings
  5. Critical reflection and further work

Marking Criteria

Component Weight What we look for
Technical implementation 30% Runs reliably on device. Competent use of the toolchain. Appropriate complexity.
Interaction and experience design 25% Deliberate, justified design choices. Comfort and usability taken seriously.
Evaluation 20% A real method, honestly applied, with findings that actually informed changes.
Critical reflection 15% Engages with module theory. Honest about limitations.
Presentation and documentation 10% Clear report and video. Professional communication.

Marking Bands

Band Descriptor
70–100 Ambitious but controlled. Runs reliably. Design choices are explicitly grounded in module theory. Evaluation is genuine and demonstrably changed the work. Reflection is critical and self-aware.
60–69 Solid, working application with clear design reasoning. Evaluation carried out competently. Reflection shows good understanding.
50–59 Application runs with limitations. Design reasoning present but thin or mostly implicit. Evaluation minimal. Reflection descriptive.
Below 50 Incomplete or non-functional. Little design rationale, no meaningful evaluation, no critical engagement.

Tips

  • Lock your scope early. The most common failure in this module is a concept too large to finish.
  • Get it onto hardware in week one of building. Deployment problems found late are fatal.
  • Test with someone who has not seen it. You cannot evaluate your own interface.
  • Write the report as you go. Design decisions are impossible to reconstruct honestly at the end.
  • Report what did not work. Honest evaluation of a flawed prototype scores better than a claim that everything worked perfectly.
