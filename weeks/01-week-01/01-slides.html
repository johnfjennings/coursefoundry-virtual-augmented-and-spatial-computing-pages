What Is XR?

Note

XR — eXtended Reality is an umbrella term for the full spectrum of immersive technologies.

It covers:

  • Virtual Reality (VR)
  • Augmented Reality (AR)
  • Mixed Reality (MR)
  • Spatial Computing

Virtual Reality

The user is placed inside a fully synthetic environment.

  • No view of the real world
  • Complete sensory substitution (visual, audio, haptic)

Examples: Meta Quest, HTC Vive Pro

Augmented Reality

Digital content is overlaid on the real world.

  • The real world remains visible
  • Digital objects appear to exist in physical space

Examples: HoloLens 2, Snap Spectacles, Pokémon GO

Mixed Reality

A spectrum between fully real and fully virtual.

  • Digital and physical objects coexist and interact

Examples: HoloLens 2, Meta Quest 3 with passthrough

Spatial Computing

A broader paradigm shift in how we interact with computers.

  • Computing moves into the environment
  • Interfaces are no longer confined to screens

Examples: Apple Vision Pro, HoloLens 2

Comparing XR Modalities

Replaces Reality

Virtual Reality
Fully synthetic — no real world visible

Extends Reality

AR / MR / Spatial Computing
Digital and physical worlds combined

Tip

Most modern XR devices sit somewhere along this spectrum.

6DOF vs 3DOF

3DOF

Tracks rotation only

  • Pitch
  • Yaw
  • Roll

6DOF

Tracks rotation AND position

  • Pitch, Yaw, Roll
  • X, Y, Z translation

Allows the user to physically move through space.

Note

All primary devices in this module are 6DOF.

Inside-Out vs Outside-In Tracking

Inside-Out

Cameras on the headset track the environment

Examples: Quest 2, Quest 3

Outside-In

External sensors track the headset

Examples: Vive Pro (base stations)

Ten Years of Hype

Where VR, AR and Spatial Computing sat on Gartner’s Hype Cycle

https://www.gartner.com/en/articles/hype-cycle-for-emerging-technologies

This section runs roughly 10 minutes. The pedagogical point is not the positions themselves but learning to read industry claims critically — which is exactly what Assessment 2 asks for.

The Hype Cycle

Gartner’s model of how expectations for a technology change over time.

The y-axis is expectations, not capability, adoption, or revenue.

Reading This Honestly

Before we plot anything on it:

  • Gartner publishes many Hype Cycles — this is only one
  • The reports are paywalled; these positions come from public commentary
  • The curve is an analyst judgement, not a measurement
  • Leaving the cycle can mean matured — or just displaced by something newer

Important

Treat what follows as a claim to evaluate, not a fact to memorise.

Virtual Reality: 2015 → 2018

  • In the trough until 2016, then onto the Slope of Enlightenment
  • 2017: on the slope, 2–5 years to plateau
  • 2018: removed — no longer “emerging”

Augmented Reality: 2016 → 2019

  • In the Trough of Disillusionment 2016–2018, 5–10 years to plateau
  • Rated further from maturity than VR throughout
  • 2019: removed, approaching maturity

The Metaverse Spike: 2022 → 2023

  • Entered 2022 at the peak, with a 10+ years to plateau estimate
  • Sliding toward the trough by 2023
  • Expectations collapsed. The hardware did not change.

Spatial Computing: 2024 → 2025

  • On the 2024 Emerging Technologies cycle, Total Experience theme
  • A Gartner 2025 strategic technology trend
  • Forecast $110bn (2023) → $1.7tn (2033)

Exact phase not public — so no point is drawn.

Ten Years, One Chart

What Leaving the Cycle Means

The most useful lesson in ten years of this chart:

  • VR and AR did not fail — they left because they matured. Removal is not a verdict of failure.
  • The Metaverse rose and fell in roughly 24 months without the underlying hardware changing at all.
  • The vocabulary churns faster than the technology. VR → AR → MR → Metaverse → Spatial Computing largely describes the same devices.

The device on your head this semester barely changed while its label moved four times.

Discussion: Hype and Evidence

  • If expectations collapsed for the Metaverse while the hardware improved, what was the chart actually measuring?
  • VR “left” the cycle in 2018 as mature. Does your experience of VR in 2026 support that?
  • Spatial computing is the current label. What would count as evidence that it is a real shift rather than a rename?
  • Whose interests are served by a technology being placed near the peak?
