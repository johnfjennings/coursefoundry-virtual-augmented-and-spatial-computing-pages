Participant Information Sheet

Study title: Comfort and Simulator Sickness Audit in XR Applications

Who is running this session?

This activity is run as part of the Virtual, Augmented and Spatial Computing module. The session lead is a member of teaching staff or a trained student facilitator acting under their supervision.

What will I be asked to do?

You will wear a VR headset and experience a short scene under several different settings (for example, different frame rates, render scales, or locomotion styles). Before and after each exposure you will complete a brief questionnaire (the Simulator Sickness Questionnaire, or SSQ) about how you feel. Each exposure lasts no more than a few minutes, with a recovery break between conditions.

Before You Begin

Please read this sheet in full before you put on a headset. If anything is unclear, ask the session facilitator before continuing.

What are the risks?

  • Simulator sickness. Some people experience nausea, dizziness, eye strain, or disorientation during or after VR use. This can happen even if you have used VR comfortably before, and even in a well-designed application.
  • Physical hazards. While wearing a headset you cannot see the room, the floor, or other people. Trips, collisions, and loss of balance are possible if the play area is not clear or if you are not spotted.
  • Delayed effects. Symptoms can appear up to several hours after removing the headset. Some researchers advise against driving immediately after a VR session if you felt unwell.

What are my rights as a participant?

  • Participation is voluntary. You may decline to take part with no explanation required and no consequence to your grade or standing.
  • You may stop at any time. You do not need a reason. Say the agreed stop word, or simply remove the headset. Stopping partway is a valid outcome and will not be treated as a failure to complete the task.
  • You may take a break at any time, including a seated recovery break, without ending your participation entirely.
  • Your data is confidential. SSQ scores and any notes are used only for the purposes of this module’s teaching and assessment activities, are anonymised where used in aggregate, and are not shared outside the module teaching team.
  • You can ask questions at any point, before, during, or after the session.

Duty of Care Protocol (Facilitator Checklist)

Before any participant puts on a headset, confirm:

Consent Statement

By signing below, I confirm that:

  1. I have read (or had read to me) the participant information sheet above.
  2. I understand the risks described, including simulator sickness and physical hazards associated with reduced awareness of my surroundings while wearing a headset.
  3. I understand that my participation is voluntary and that I may withdraw at any time, for any reason, without needing to explain myself.
  4. I understand that stopping early is a valid and acceptable outcome, not a failure.
  5. I understand how any data collected (such as SSQ scores) will be used and stored.
  6. I have had the opportunity to ask questions and am satisfied with the answers given.

I consent to take part in this activity on the terms described above.

NoteFor Facilitators

Retain a copy of this signed form for the duration of the module. If a participant reports symptoms after the session that cause concern, direct them to student health services and notify the module coordinator.

WarningWithdrawal of Consent

A participant may withdraw consent at any point, including after a session has ended. If this happens, destroy any data collected for that participant and record only that a withdrawal occurred, with no further detail required.
