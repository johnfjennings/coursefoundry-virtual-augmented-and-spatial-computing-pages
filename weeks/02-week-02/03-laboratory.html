Week 2 Lab: The XR Hardware Landscape — Device Stations and Critical Comparison

Lab Overview

This lab is a structured, hands-on survey of the current XR hardware landscape, spanning standalone VR, mixed reality headsets, and lightweight AR glasses. You will experience each category directly, then work in a group to critically evaluate devices against a shared set of evaluation criteria.

By the end of this lab you should be able to:

  • describe the key differences between standalone VR, passthrough MR, optical see-through MR, and AR glasses
  • identify how hardware constraints (optics, tracking, form factor) shape user experience
  • apply a structured evaluation framework (comfort, FOV, tracking fidelity, ecosystem maturity) to real devices
  • defend a comparative judgement about device suitability for a given use case

Device Stations

The lab is organised into stations, grouped by the three hardware categories from this week’s lecture. Spend approximately 15–20 minutes at each station available to you.

Station Device Category Focus
A Meta Quest 3 / 3S Standalone VR Pancake lenses, passthrough, hand tracking
B Apple Vision Pro Mixed reality (passthrough) visionOS interaction model, eye + pinch input
C HoloLens 2 Mixed reality (optical see-through) Waveguide optics, narrow FOV, hand/gaze tracking
D Xreal One Pro (or similar) AR glasses Tethered display, birdbath/waveguide optics, spatial anchoring
NoteIf a device isn’t physically available

Not every module will have hands-on access to every device (Vision Pro and Xreal glasses in particular may be limited-availability loan units). Where a device isn’t available, use the manufacturer’s published specifications, official demo videos, and at least one independent third-party review to complete the observation template as a desk-based evaluation, and note clearly in your submission that this station was evaluated second-hand rather than hands-on.

Observation Template

Use this template for each device you evaluate, hands-on or desk-based.

Device Name

Category: Standalone VR / MR (passthrough) / MR (optical see-through) / AR glasses

Tracking: Inside-out / Outside-in / Host-device dependent

Standalone: Yes / No (tethered to: ______)

Comfort

  • How heavy does the device feel, and how is weight distributed?
  • Is there any pressure on the face, nose, or head strap?
  • How long could you realistically wear it before discomfort sets in?

Field of View (FOV)

  • How wide does the field of view feel?
  • Where does content clip or disappear at the edges?
  • Does the FOV feel adequate for the device’s intended use case?

Tracking Fidelity

  • Does head/hand/controller tracking feel accurate and low-latency?
  • Is there any drift, jitter, or loss of tracking?
  • What happens when you move quickly, or into low light / low-texture environments?

Interaction Model

  • What are the primary input methods (controllers, hand tracking, eye + pinch, touch)?
  • How natural or learnable does the interaction feel?
  • Are there noticeable latency or precision issues?

Ecosystem Maturity

  • What store/distribution model does this device use?
  • How mature does the app catalogue seem (breadth, quality, update frequency)?
  • How accessible is the SDK/developer documentation, based on this week’s lecture and practical?

Guided Tasks at Each Station

Station A: Meta Quest 3 / 3S

  1. Compare the Quest 3’s pancake lenses to the Quest 3S’s Fresnel lenses if both are available — note clarity and headset depth differences.
  2. Enable passthrough and observe colour fidelity and latency between head movement and the passthrough image.
  3. Try hand tracking versus controller-based interaction for the same simple task. Which feels more reliable?
  4. Note the FOV — where does the image cut off at the periphery?

Station B: Apple Vision Pro

  1. Complete the eye and hand calibration process.
  2. Navigate the visionOS home environment using only eye gaze + pinch.
  3. Open a window/volume and observe how it anchors in physical space as you move.
  4. Compare the passthrough image quality and latency to the Quest 3’s.

Station C: HoloLens 2

  1. Put on the headset and complete the eye calibration.
  2. Open a holographic application and note where the holographic content disappears — this is the FOV boundary.
  3. Try hand tracking and, if available, gaze + voice interaction.
  4. Compare the “real world” view (direct optical) to the Quest 3/Vision Pro’s camera-based passthrough. Which feels more natural, and why?

Station D: Xreal One Pro (or similar AR glasses)

  1. Put on the glasses and connect to the host device (phone/PC/puck).
  2. Note the size, position, and clarity of the projected virtual screen.
  3. If spatial anchoring is supported, move your head and observe whether the screen stays fixed in space or moves with your head.
  4. Compare the form factor and social conspicuousness to the headset-class devices at the other stations.

Critical Comparison Activity (Group Work)

Form groups of 3–4. Each group selects 2–3 devices from the stations above (or from the wider market if your group wants to include a device not present in the lab, e.g. PICO 4 Ultra, Samsung Galaxy XR).

  1. Using your completed comparison table and this week’s lecture criteria — comfort, FOV, tracking fidelity, and ecosystem maturity — produce a ranked evaluation of your chosen devices for a specific use case your group selects (e.g. “classroom-based language learning,” “warehouse pick-and-pack training,” “casual media consumption on a commute,” “competitive VR esports”).
  2. For each criterion, justify your ranking with a specific observation from today’s stations or documented specifications — not just a gut impression.
  3. Identify at least one criterion where the “best” device for your use case is not the most technically impressive device overall. Explain why.
  4. Prepare a 3-minute group summary to present to the class.
TipWhat makes a strong evaluation

A weak evaluation says “Vision Pro felt more premium.” A strong evaluation says “Vision Pro’s passthrough had noticeably less colour distortion than the Quest 3’s, which matters for our use case because accurate colour reproduction is essential for a design-review application — but its higher price and single-vendor ecosystem make Quest 3 a stronger choice for a classroom of 30 students needing devices at scale.”

Reflection Questions

Answer these individually in your lab notes after the group activity.

  1. Which device felt most immersive, and which criterion (comfort, FOV, tracking, ecosystem) mattered most to that judgement?
  2. Where did optical see-through (HoloLens 2) versus passthrough (Quest 3, Vision Pro) make the most noticeable difference to your experience?
  3. How did the AR glasses form factor change your expectations of what the device should be able to do, compared to the headsets?
  4. Did your group’s ranking change once you factored in ecosystem maturity, rather than hardware specs alone? Why or why not?
  5. For which use case would you choose each of the four device categories, and why?

Lab Deliverable

Submit your completed observation templates, comparison table, and your group’s critical comparison write-up (ranking + justification for your chosen use case).

This contributes to Assessment 1: Continuous Lab Work.

Extension Task

If time allows:

  • Research the specifications of one device not covered in this lab (e.g. Samsung Galaxy XR, PICO 4 Ultra, Snap Spectacles 2024)
  • Slot it into your group’s comparison table and re-run your ranking — does it change your recommendation?
